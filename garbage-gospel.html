Answer B

Attorneys who file AI-generated citations without verifying them may face Rule 11 sanctions. See Mata v. Avianca, Inc., 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023), where the court imposed a monetary penalty and required notice to the judges falsely named as authors.

Real. Judge Castel's order is the decision that put this problem on every bar's radar.

The mechanism

It predicts the next plausible word. It never checks a source.

Pattern 1

Fabricated citations

A case name, volume, page and year assembled from the shape of real citations.

Pattern 2

Invented facts

A deposition date, a dollar figure, a party's role, filled in because the sentence needed one.

Pattern 3

Quiet omissions

Ask for a summary of 40 documents. It quietly skips three. One of them was the email that changes the case.

Pattern 4

Agreeable answers

Ask "confirm that X," and it tends to confirm X. Your premise becomes its conclusion.

The two-way street

Make it ask you before it answers.

One sentence in the prompt turns a guesser into an interviewer.

YouDraft a privilege log from the attached email export. Before you begin, ask me up to three questions you need answered to do this well. Do not start until I answer.
AIBefore I start:
  1. Which attorneys and legal staff should I treat as "counsel" for privilege purposes? Please list names and roles.
  2. Should I flag emails where a third party is copied, since that may waive privilege?
  3. When I can't tell whether an email is legal advice or business advice, should I mark it "review" rather than decide?
YouGood questions. Yes to 2 and 3. Counsel list attached.

Ask it to interrogate you

"What are you assuming that I haven't told you?"

Ask what it didn't find

"List what you looked for and could not find in this material."

Ask for confidence, per item

"For each row, say how sure you are and what I'd have to check to be certain."

0:24 to 0:29 - The exchange animates in on its own over about five seconds: your prompt, the AI's three questions, your reply, then the three follow-up prompts. Pause and let it play. - Step 1: read the bold sentence aloud. That's the whole trick. - Step 2: read question 3 aloud. That's the machine asking permission to say "I don't know." You want that. - Every clarifying question it asks is a fact you would otherwise have had to notice was missing after the fact. - Field question you'll get: "Doesn't this take longer?" Yes, two minutes. Compare to one sanctions hearing.

It should say when it could not read something

Ask any tool, every time: "Which pages could you not read, and why?" The honest answers:

Bad scanA 3 becomes an 8.
WatermarkStamped over the words it needed.
HandwritingNotes and filled-in forms.
Locked PDFComes through as zero pages.
RedactionsA gap, not an answer.

If an answer rests on one of these pages, note it: "source partially unreadable, verified against the original."

0:34 to 0:44 · Demo 1 and 2 - Switch to the demo laptop. Pick a case already loaded. - Ask a natural question about a participant or a date. Show the streamed answer with [1] [2]. - Click a citation card. Show session, date, direction, Bates, and open the PDF page. That page is the whole difference: the answer leads back to paper. - Ask a structured question too ("list sessions on 3/14 by participant") so they see it can also just query the data. - Then ask a question you know the file cannot answer ("What is Mike's home address?"). Demo

It checks its own work. You still check it too.

The rules from slide 8 are promises. This is how the tool catches itself breaking them, before you see the answer.

Demo laptop · run an investigation, open the trace
[N]
Does every claim point at a real source?If the answer cites source [7] and there were only five, the answer gets flagged. If it cites nothing at all, that gets flagged too.
✓
Does the source actually say that?For a written brief, each sentence is compared against the page it cites. A sentence that sounds right but isn't on that page gets caught. That is the exact mistake from the sanctions cases.
≠
Do the documents disagree with each other?Two pages giving different dates for the same event get surfaced for a person to sort out, instead of the tool quietly picking one.
#
Can someone else retrace it?An investigation exports as a package: every step taken, every citation used, and a fingerprint of each source file. Someone can check the work a year later.

In supervised mode it stops and asks you before it plans, before it writes, and whenever the documents conflict. The person stays in charge.
